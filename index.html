BEFORE YOU BREAK GROUND

A LITTLE FIELD GUIDE · 02

When should you call a land use planner?

Honestly? Earlier than you'd think.

Before you buy land, hire a designer, draw up plans, or pour a foundation, it's worth knowing what the land allows. A few hours of research now can save you thousands of dollars and a lot of heartbreak later.

  • You're thinking about buying rural property.
  • You have an unconventional building idea.
  • You want to build with cob, straw bale, or other natural materials.
  • You're not sure what your zoning allows.
  • A city or county gave you confusing or conflicting answers.
  • You want to know your project is feasible before paying for plans.
  • You need help understanding the approval process.

Good homework up front is the cheapest part of any project.

A LITTLE ABOUT ME · 05

Land is more than
what's on paper.

And planning can be personal.

I'm an artist, writer, builder, and land use planner. I grew up in apartment complexes, and as an adult I've lived in communal, permaculture-minded homesteads and ecovillages. Those places taught me how beautiful, human-scale, and healing it can be to live close to the land. They also showed me how hard it is to get there if you don't already own property.

I believe living on the land should be a reciprocal relationship. Every time we build, we leave a footprint. But when we build with care, we can make something that nourishes our families, our communities, and the ground itself.

I left conventional planning because I couldn't keep working on projects that were only trying to be cheap, standard, and profitable. Now I'm back, on my own terms, to work with people on small, earth-conscious projects. I'm also building my own hands-on skills in cob, permaculture, and natural building, so I can bring more than regulations to the table.

My goal is to make these projects more accessible, which is why I offer reduced rates to people who might not otherwise be able to get this kind of help.
